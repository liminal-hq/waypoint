# Accessibility and Focus Review

Status: prototype review, 2026-09-29. Findings are from reading the markup and behaviour, not from testing with a screen reader yet.

## What is in place

- **Landmarks:** the tab strip is a tablist (each tab has aria-selected), the toolbar is a toolbar, the sidebar is a navigation region, the file area is the main region, and the status bar is a polite live region.
- **Menu bar and Action bar:** the application menu is a menu button (`aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded`) opened by F10, a lone Alt or Alt plus F, E, V or W (F10 and a lone Alt toggle it, so pressing either again closes it), with arrow keys, Escape and focus returning to the button; disabled rows are `aria-disabled` with the reason as their title. The Action bar is a `toolbar` with a roving tab stop (Left, Right, Home, End), its buttons are 28 px targets named by their label (also when labels are hidden), a disabled button stays focusable, is `aria-disabled` and is described by its reason, and it moves its trailing buttons into a More menu rather than clipping them. It only transitions when motion is allowed.
- **Command palette (milestone 4, slice 20):** a native modal `dialog` named “Command Palette” holding a `combobox` (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls` the list, `aria-activedescendant` the active option, `aria-autocomplete="list"`) and a `listbox` of `option`s with `aria-selected`; focus stays in the field while the arrow keys, Home, End, PageUp and PageDown move the active option, and typing always goes to the field (Tab moves nothing). An option is named by one sentence (“Move to Trash, File, shortcut Delete”, with “on” or “off” for a setting and “unavailable: reason” for a disabled command), and the match is bold as well as the active row outlined, so neither is shown by colour alone. A polite live region reads the count (“12 commands”, “No commands match”) and, for Enter on a disabled command, the reason. Esc, a click outside and the window losing focus close it and focus returns to where it was; a command that moves focus (Rename) runs after that, so it keeps it. Animation is off under Reduce motion. The key is Ctrl+Shift+P, which does not clash with text editing.
- **Keyboard:** every action has a keyboard path (see the shortcut list, press ?). Shortcuts can be rebound in Settings → Keyboard, with conflict detection.
- **Focus:** a setting turns on a thick, always-visible focus ring.
- **Motion:** a setting turns off all animation and transitions, including drag feedback.
- **Contrast:** a high-contrast setting strengthens borders and lifts muted text. Transparency keeps a solid backing behind text.
- **Touch:** touch mode raises rows, buttons and menu items to at least 44 pixels; Auto turns it on in narrow windows.
- **Text size:** Default, Large (115%), Larger (130%).
- **Direction:** right-to-left mirrors the window, tabs, sidebar and back/forward arrows. `lang` and `dir` are set on each window's root from the language (Settings → Language & region; the Direction row can force either way). Layout uses CSS logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, `inset-inline-end`, `text-align: start`), enforced by `scripts/check-logical-css.sh`, which lists the places where physical is right with a `physical:` comment (the tab drag pill and split zones that follow the pointer, the context menu's viewport placement). Directional glyphs (back, forward, the small chevron, undo, redo, copy to, move to, the sidebar glyph) carry `data-directional` and flip under `dir=rtl`; a glyph that means a physical side, such as the tab strip's scroll buttons, opts out.
- **Arrow keys follow the direction:** in a right-to-left layout Left is forward and Right is back in the grid and grouped lists, the folder tree (Left opens a folder), the Action bar, tabs and groups (including Ctrl+Shift+arrow reorder), Quick Look, and menus (Left opens a submenu), as they already did in the Inspector, the Shelf, pane dividers and segmented rows. Handlers use `isRtl` and `inlineKey` in `i18n/direction.ts`. Scrollers use `scrollLeft` as signed in right-to-left.
- **Checking a layout:** a developer build offers the pseudo-locales `en-XA` (accented, 30% longer, `[` `]` markers) and `ar-XB` (right to left); choose `ar-XB` and look for anything that did not mirror, or clipped text under `en-XA`. Reordering tabs by dragging is not yet mirrored in a right-to-left layout; the keyboard reorder is.

## Still to do (found in this review)

1. **File rows need roles.** Rows are plain elements. Give the list role=listbox with aria-multiselectable, each row role=option with aria-selected, and announce the selection count.
2. **Menus.** Context menus need role=menu and menuitem, arrow-key focus, and to return focus to the trigger on close. The optional menu bar (Settings → General → Title bar) is a `role=menubar` of `menuitem` buttons with `aria-haspopup="menu"`, `aria-expanded` and `aria-keyshortcuts` (Alt+F, E, V, W), and one tab stop. Left, Right, Home and End move along it, Down, Enter or Space open a menu with its first row focused, and Escape closes it with focus back on its button; while a menu is open Left and Right switch to the neighbouring one. F10 or a lone Alt focuses the first menu and, pressed again or followed by Escape, returns focus to the element it came from. It collapses into a More menu rather than clipping, and its background transition is off under reduced motion. While it is shown the title bar's app name is plain text, not a second menu button.
3. **Dialogs.** Give them role=dialog with a label, trap focus, and restore it on close. Escape already closes them.
   - The shared `Dialog` (`docs/shared-components.md`) gives role=dialog (native) named by its title, focus into the dialog on open (never on a destructive button), a Tab and Shift+Tab trap, Esc, focus restored to the opener, and a non-dismissible mode for questions that must be answered.
   - Checklist for each dialog the app builds on it: the title says what the dialog is about; destructive confirmations use `ConfirmDialog` with `danger`; a dialog that cannot be dismissed offers a visible way out or an answer to every question; stacked dialogs return focus to the one beneath.
   - Conflict and error dialogs (milestone 4, slice 09): the conflict dialog is a `role=dialog` named by its count and destination, with a table (caption, column headers, each name a row header) whose choice selects are named “Choice for {name}” and described by what the choice does, so the danger of Replace is said in words (“Replaces the whole folder…”) as well as styled. Nothing is pre-selected; Continue is a disabled button until every clash is answered, and a polite status line (“2 of 4 answered”) says how far the answers are. Focus starts on the first control (the bulk choice, empty), never a destructive one. Escape closes the dialog and leaves the job waiting, exactly like Decide later; “Cancel the operation” stops the job, behind a confirmation whose default focus is “Keep deciding” once anything was answered; a click outside does nothing. The error dialog is named “An item could not be processed” (or “The original folder is gone”), describes the problem as its description, starts on Retry (Recreate folders for a missing folder), never on Skip all like this, and keeps checksums behind a disclosure; the item and job lines are selectable text. After Continue the live region says what will happen (“Continuing: Skip 1, Keep both 1”).
4. **Drag and drop.** Provide a non-pointer path for every drop: Copy To… and Move To… cover files; the tab menu covers splitting. Announce drop targets and results in the live region.
   - File drags (milestone 4, slice 12): every outcome has a path without a pointer, listed in `NON_POINTER_PATHS` (`dnd/fileDragModel.ts`) and checked by `fileDragParity.test.tsx`: Copy To…, Move To…, Link To… (in the Edit menu and the palette, with no key), Move to Trash (Delete), Open in New Tab, Paste Into Folder and F5 for a folder, a pane and the other pane, and the destination dialog for a place, a tab or a breadcrumb. The drag says "Dragging 3 items" when it starts, "Over Documents: will move" (or why it is not allowed) as the target changes, "Opened docs" and "Returned to where the drag began" for a spring, "Drag cancelled" on Esc, and the result comes from the queue (the toast and its Undo). The ghost and pill are `aria-hidden` because the live region says the same words. The action picker is the shared context menu named "Drop action" (arrows, Enter, Esc), and closing it without a choice says "Drop cancelled". A refusal is shown by words and a dashed edge as well as colour, and under Reduce motion the shake and the spring ring's animation are off.Files dragged in from other applications use the same words ("Dragging 3 items", "Over Documents: will copy", "The files left the window"), a drag that continues as the system's says "Dragging notes.txt out of the window" and its end is read out ("Dropped…", "Moved … to another application", "Drag cancelled"), and a refusal to start it is a notice. These drags have no keyboard path of their own: Copy, Cut and Paste with the system clipboard do the same (`NATIVE_DROP_PATHS`, `OUTBOUND_PATHS`).
5. **Progress.** Done for the queue (milestone 4, slice 06): the status bar ring is a button named by its state ("Operations, 2 in progress, 50% complete, waiting for you"), with `aria-expanded` and `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, and colour is never the only signal (the count, the label and a dot with the state in the name). The popover is a non-modal `role=dialog` named "Operations": it opens with Enter or Space, puts focus on the first job row (or on the panel when the list is empty), closes with Escape and returns focus to the ring, and closes without taking focus when a press or Tab lands outside. The rows are a list with one tab stop (Up, Down, Home and End move between rows; Alt+Up and Alt+Down reorder a queued job and announce the new position); each row is named by its title and described by its state, and its buttons are named "Action: title". The live region speaks a job starting (once), waiting for the person, failing, being cancelled and finishing, with how many others are still in progress, and 25, 50 and 75 % of a job that has run three seconds, never every tick. The ring does not animate (a job not yet sized is a still quarter, not a spinner) and its fill transition is off under reduced motion. Still to do: a screen reader pass, and the Operations window's own focus order.
   - Inline rename (milestone 4, slice 07): the field is named "Rename name" and described by a hint ("Enter renames, Escape cancels.") and by a polite live region under it that holds what is wrong with the name or why Rust refused it, so a taken name is read out as it appears; `aria-invalid` follows. The extension question is the shared `Dialog` with "Keep .jpg" focused and Escape returning to the field. When the field ends, focus returns to the file list (whose active descendant is the renamed row), and a finished rename is announced ("Renamed a.txt to b.txt"). A new folder or file opens in the field with its default name selected, so the result of F7 is spoken as the field's name. Delete Permanently's confirmation lists the items and starts on Cancel; refusals (read-only location, nothing selected, nothing to undo, an undo that cannot be done and why) are said through the toast's live region, never dropped.
   - The Shelf (milestone 4, slice 14): dropping on the Shelf is reached without a pointer by Add to Shelf in the file menu, the Edit menu and the palette (`NON_POINTER_PATHS.shelf`), and dragging out by Copy and Paste or Copy To… on the item (Ctrl+C in the panel puts the files on the clipboard). The panel is a landmark named "Shelf" holding a tree (`aria-multiselectable`, groups at level 1 and items at level 2, `aria-activedescendant` for the row the keyboard is on) with Up, Down, Home, End, Left, Right, Enter, Space, Ctrl+A, Esc, Delete, Ctrl+C and the Menu key; its edge is a separator with `aria-valuenow` that the arrow keys move. Removal is announced ("Removed 2 items from the Shelf"), added items are said in the status toast, a full Shelf says so, and "Missing" is a word beside a dashed border, not only a dimmed row. The drag pill reads "Add 3 items to the Shelf" in the live region and refusals give their reason as for any drag.
   - Settings window (milestone 4, slice 18): the pages are the shared `SettingsShell` (a `nav` of section buttons with `aria-current` and a roving tab stop, a `main` named by its heading, groups named by their titles). Every row's control is named by its label and described by its description; a toggle is a `switch`, a choice of two or three is a `radiogroup`, and a number field has its unit beside it. A change applies at once, so there is no Save button to find; a refusal appears under its row as an `alert`, the field is marked `aria-invalid`, and it keeps the value that is in force. A row that depends on another is disabled and says what to turn on in its description (the word "Unavailable" is kept for what a system cannot do, such as dragging files out to other applications, which the Drag & drop page explains in text). `Ctrl+,` opens the window from any main window.
6. **Focus order.** Title bar, tab strip, toolbar, action bar, sidebar, files, inspector, status bar. Confirm with a keyboard-only pass.
7. **Colour is never the only signal.** Git status has letters; tags need names in the row tooltip; connection dots need text alternatives.
8. **Icon-only buttons** (view switcher, joint, action bar in narrow windows) need accessible names. Most have a title; add aria-labels.
9. **Pointer-only gestures** (long-press history, hold-to-split) need keyboard equivalents. Alt+Left/Right with a menu key, and Split With in the tab menu, cover them.
10. **Minimum sizes:** text stays at or above 12 px; touch mode covers 44 px targets.
